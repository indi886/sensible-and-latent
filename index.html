Sensible heat · what the thermostat reads

It is 112 °F on the other side of this glass.

Sensible heat changes the temperature of the air. The sun on a west window, an uninsulated attic, a dark roof in July: a thermometer sees all of it, and every installer sizes for it.

Latent heat · what it doesn't

The water on this side is the other half of the load.

Latent heat is the moisture in the air. In a monsoon August it can be a third of a Tucson cooling load, and no thermostat on the wall will show it to you. An oversized system cools the air in eight minutes and shuts off before the coil has pulled any water out of it. That's the clammy, cold-and-sticky house.

The install · one house in Tucson, start to finish

Day 0 09:00

  1. 01

    Survey

    Measure the house, not the old unit.

    Every window, orientation, insulation level and leak goes into a room-by-room Manual J. The old 4-ton nameplate is not an input.

  2. 02

    Design

    Size the equipment and the air.

    Manual S picks a 2.5-ton variable-speed heat pump. Manual D lays out an 18 × 10 in trunk and eight branches, each sized to its room's load.

  3. 03

    Ductwork

    Hang it, seal it, test it.

    Trunk sections, takeoffs, boots and flex runs on straps every four feet. Mastic on every seam, then a duct-blaster test before a single register goes in.

  4. 04

    Equipment

    Set the condenser, level the air handler.

    The outdoor unit goes on a 36 × 36 in pad, 3 in above grade with 18 in of clearance. The air handler goes in the hall closet on a vibration pad.

  5. 05

    Line set

    Braze under nitrogen.

    Six joints are brazed with 15% silver while dry nitrogen flows at 3 SCFH. No scale forms inside the pipe to clog the metering device a year from now.

  6. 06

    Test & evacuate

    Pressure first, then vacuum.

    Nitrogen to 450 psig for 30 minutes, no drop. Then a vacuum under 500 microns, isolated. If it rises past 1,000 µ in ten minutes, we find out why.

  7. 07

    Charge & start

    Weigh it in, then prove it.

    Factory charge plus the line-set adder, weighed on a scale. Then we check it against subcooling at the coil, not against a guess.

  8. 08

    Air on

    Commission it, and hand over the numbers.

    Supply and return temperatures, static pressure and airflow at every register go into a report you keep. Then the sun goes down, and the house stays at 75.

Questions · read like a service manual

Symptom → check → answer

Only if the house still needs 4 tons, and most don't. Windows, insulation and duct leaks have changed since the old one went in, and plenty were oversized on day one. An oversized system short-cycles: it cools the air but never runs long enough to pull the moisture out. We run a room-by-room Manual J and size to that.

In Tucson, almost always a heat pump. Our winter design temperature is 32 °F, and a modern variable-speed heat pump delivers its rated heat below that at two and a half to three and a half times the efficiency of resistance heat. Dual fuel makes sense when gas is already in the house and you want to keep it.

R-454B is the lower-GWP refrigerant new equipment has used since the 2025 EPA transition. Your R-410A system can be serviced for the rest of its life, and the refrigerant stays available. The two can't be mixed. New A2L equipment needs a leak-detection sensor in the air handler, and we install it and test it.

Usually airflow, not equipment: an undersized or leaking branch duct, no return path when the door is shut, or a register throwing air the wrong way. We measure airflow at each register with a flow hood and fix the duct before we suggest anything bigger.

A like-for-like heat pump swap takes one day. With new ductwork it takes two to three. We pressure test, evacuate and commission on the last day, and you have the report before we leave the driveway.

Twice a year in Tucson: in April before the cooling season and in October before heating. Haboob dust loads the outdoor coil, and condensate lines clog with algae in monsoon season. Both cost you capacity long before anything breaks.

Utility rebates for qualifying heat pumps change every year. We check what applies to your exact model and house on the day we quote, and we file the paperwork. Financing is available on approved credit, with the terms written on the quote.
